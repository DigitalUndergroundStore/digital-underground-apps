The free-to-paid ladder: how to turn a free download into your first sale

Digital UndergroundOctober 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Most indie makers have the same problem: people will take something free, but nobody buys. The fix is rarely "more traffic". It is usually a missing ladder: a clear path from a free thing someone already wanted to one paid thing that is the natural next step. This guide shows how to build one in a weekend, using our own setup as the worked example.

Rung 1: one free thing that is actually good

The free item has to be worth keeping on its own. A cut-down demo that ends on a paywall feels like a trick. A complete, useful thing builds trust. Good options:

  • A full first book in a series, or the first chapter pack of a course.
  • A free beta of an app with real, working features.
  • A template, checklist or starter kit that solves one small job completely.

Our example: Book 1 of the Humanity X series is free with email signup as a full novel (PDF, EPUB and Word), with an audiobook version free for a limited time.

Rung 2: ask for the email, and say exactly what they get

An email address is the only part of the funnel you own. Social reach can disappear overnight; your list does not. Keep the signup honest:

  • One field, one button, and a plain sentence about what you will send.
  • Deliver instantly on the success screen, not "check your inbox in 10 minutes".
  • Make consent for marketing a separate checkbox, and include an unsubscribe link in every email.

Rung 3: put the paid next step where the free thing ends

The highest-intent moment is right after someone finishes or downloads the free item. That is where the paid step belongs:

  • On the success screen: "Enjoying it? Here is the next one" with the price shown.
  • Inside the file: a last page or README pointing to the next product.
  • In the first follow-up email: one link, one reason, one price.

In our case, the free book ends with a "Next: Into the Stars" page and the series page shows Book 2 with its price next to the free Book 1. No countdown timers, no fake "only 3 left". Buyers can tell, and it costs you trust.

Rung 4: one paid product, priced plainly

Do not offer five options at this point. Offer the single most obvious continuation (Book 2, the Pro version, the full template pack) at a clear one-time price, with a one-line refund policy. If you want help choosing the number, see how to price your side project.

Rung 5: a bundle for people who are already sure

Once the ladder works, add a bundle for buyers who want everything at once. It is the easiest upsell to explain because it saves them money and clicks. Add it last; it does not fix a ladder with no first paid rung.

Common mistakes

  • The free thing is the paid thing with pieces missing. People feel baited and leave.
  • No price anywhere near the free download. If buyers cannot see the next step, they will not take it.
  • Too many links. One free item, one paid next step. Everything else can wait.
  • Checkout on a page you do not control. If a platform changes its rules, your ladder breaks. See why to own your checkout.

A weekend checklist

  1. Pick your free item and make sure it is complete on its own.
  2. Add a one-field signup that delivers it instantly.
  3. Add a "what's next" block, with the price, to the success screen and inside the file.
  4. Write one follow-up email with one link.
  5. Put the free item and the paid next step side by side on one page you can share everywhere.
