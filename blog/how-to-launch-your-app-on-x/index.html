How to launch your app on X: a step-by-step plan for indie makers

Digital UndergroundOctober 27, 2026 · 5 min read

If you are figuring out how to launch your app on X, you have probably noticed that the advice online assumes you already have thousands of followers. Most indie makers do not. The good news is that a launch on X is less about one viral post and more about a few weeks of preparation, a clear launch day, and steady follow-up. This guide walks through each part.

Why X works for indie launches

X is one of the few places where makers, early adopters and other builders all hang out in public. People there are used to seeing new projects, replying to founders directly and reposting things they find useful. You can reach someone who has never heard of you through a single repost. The flip side is that the feed moves fast, so a launch that is only one post on one day tends to disappear within hours.

Step 1: Get your profile ready before anything else

Everyone who sees your launch post and is curious will tap your profile first. Make sure it answers their questions in a few seconds:

  • Bio: say what you build and for whom, in plain words. "Building a meal planner for busy parents" beats a list of buzzwords.
  • Link: point to the app itself or a single page that lists everything you make, not a link tree with ten options.
  • Pinned post: before launch, pin something that shows the product, such as a short screen recording. After launch, pin the launch post.
  • Header image: a simple screenshot or product name is enough. It just needs to match what you are promoting.

Step 2: Warm up for two weeks

A launch post from an account that has been silent for months usually goes nowhere. Spend the two weeks before launch being visibly part of the conversation:

  • Reply more than you post. Find people who talk about the problem your app solves, and other makers in your niche, and leave thoughtful replies. A good reply on a bigger account's post is often seen by more people than your own posts.
  • Share the build. Post a few short updates: a screenshot of a feature, a decision you made and why, a bug that taught you something. These give people a reason to follow before there is anything to buy.
  • Ask for input. Posts that ask a real question ("Which of these two onboarding screens is clearer?") invite replies, and replies help the post spread.
  • Tell people the date. A few days before launch, say when it goes live. People who were interested will look for it.

Step 3: Prepare your launch assets

Have everything ready the day before, so launch day is about talking to people, not editing images.

  • A short demo video. Fifteen to forty-five seconds of the app doing its main job, recorded from the real product. Add captions, since many people scroll with the sound off.
  • Two or three screenshots as a backup or for follow-up posts.
  • A landing page that matches the post. If the post says "plan your week in five minutes", the page should say the same thing at the top, with a visible price and one clear button.
  • A launch offer, if you want one. A small, honest incentive with a real end date, such as an early-buyer price for the first week, gives people a reason to act now.

Step 4: Write a launch post people want to share

The launch post has one job: make the right people stop scrolling and understand what you built. A structure that works well:

  1. Open with the problem or the result, not with "I'm excited to announce". For example: "I kept forgetting what was in my freezer, so I built an app that tracks it in two taps."
  2. Show it. Attach the demo video or a clear screenshot directly to the post.
  3. Say who it is for and what it costs. People trust a launch more when the price is upfront.
  4. Ask for one thing. Try it, share it, or give feedback. Pick one.

Keep the main post short. If you want to tell the story behind it, put that in a short thread underneath. Many makers put the link in the first reply rather than the main post, because posts without links often travel further. Either way, make sure the link is easy to find.

Step 5: Show up on launch day

  • Post when your audience is awake. Look at when your past posts got the most replies and launch in that window.
  • Reply to every comment for the first few hours. Replies keep the conversation going and show the post is active.
  • Message a few people personally. Not a mass DM blast, but a note to the handful of people who gave you feedback during the build, letting them know it is live.
  • Quote your own post later in the day with something new: a first piece of feedback, a fix you shipped, or a different clip.

Step 6: Keep momentum after day one

Most of the value from a launch on X comes in the weeks after. Plan a few follow-up posts in advance:

  • What you learned from launch week, honestly, including what did not work.
  • A feature people asked for, shipped and shown.
  • A short walkthrough of one use case you did not cover in the launch.
  • Questions back to your users about what to build next.

Keep replying in your niche every day. The habit that warmed you up before launch is the same one that keeps new people finding you afterwards.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Tagging big accounts who do not know you and asking them to share. It rarely works and can come across as spam.
  • Posting the same launch text over and over. Each follow-up should add something new.
  • Hiding the price or requiring signup before people can see what the app does.
  • Treating it as one day. A launch is a few weeks of consistent, useful posting, with one day that is a little louder.
